# PSO, routing baselines, and ambulance dispatch

Dijkstra and A* minimize travel time for one fixed traffic snapshot. A* multiplies Haversine distance to the target by the smallest observed ratio of edge travel time to straight-line edge distance. This scales the heuristic to seconds and makes it a lower bound by the triangle inequality. Both route methods include snapshot preparation in the measured runtime.

The route PSO adapts Kennedy and Eberhart's personal-best and swarm-best updates to an integer route index and applies an inertia weight to the previous velocity. Candidate routes are the first 24 loop-free routes ranked by distance through open roads. Each particle's position refers to one candidate. Its velocity is bounded and its next position is rounded to an integer and clipped to the candidate range. Fitness is the sum of traffic-adjusted travel times. The distance-based candidate limit bounds the search and can exclude the globally fastest route under congestion. [Kennedy and Eberhart, *Particle Swarm Optimization* (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICNN.1995.488968)

Dispatch computes feasible ambulance-to-incident and incident-to-hospital legs with Dijkstra. A swarm particle encodes one ambulance/hospital choice or an unassigned marker per incident. The decoder repairs resource conflicts by trying another reachable pair when the chosen ambulance is already used or the hospital has filled. The objective first maximizes the number served, then minimizes total response time, then total transport time. Hospital beds and ambulance availability are simulated inputs. Maxwell et al. show how ambulance availability and road travel times inform fleet decisions in a dynamic EMS simulation. Their study addresses redeployment. This module addresses a batch of current incidents. [Maxwell et al., *Approximate Dynamic Programming for Ambulance Redeployment* (2010)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/ijoc.1090.0345)

Tuning used four source–target pairs from a deterministic 50-node grid with simulated congestion and road closures. Sixteen parameter combinations were tested for 20 iterations at three seeds per pair. The selected settings were inertia 0.4, cognitive coefficient 2.0, social coefficient 1.0, and six particles. Mean travel time was 2.80% above Dijkstra during tuning and 0.49% above Dijkstra across six runs on two held-out pairs from the same graph. The CSV records each configuration and mean elapsed time in milliseconds. Results characterize the synthetic prototype. Testing on the Islamabad/Rawalpindi road network requires the team's graph and traffic snapshot.
